# TAL product and positioning

This file is the record the page is built from. It is written before the
page is touched and the page is checked against it, never the other way
round. A change the record cannot account for is a change to the record
first, stated as such.

## What TAL is

**AI can only run what’s written down.** Most of how a company works never
was; an AI pilot gets the written part and stalls on the rest. **TAL makes
organizations explicit**: from the authority at the top to the code
underneath.

TAL is a company that consults and builds products. The products and the
consulting are one body of work: the products are the problems met in every
organization, solved once and given away; the engagements apply the same
thinking inside one organization.

## The seed

Everything on the page grows from one model, and every sentence on the page
must be derivable from it:

An organization is a set of decisions. What is written of a decision is who
may make it, on which facts, up to where. What is not written a person
supplies by judgment as the case arrives. An agent can hold only the written
part. Handing work to agents is the conversion of judgment into what is
written, one decision at a time, and only the person whose judgment it is
can do it, where it is safe to say that it differs from the paper. A limit is
where the conversion stops: inside it the written rule acts, past it judgment
resumes with a person, which is why authority can be handed over and taken
back by moving the line.

The positions are the parts of the seed. The cycle is the conversion for one
stream. The works are the instruments it is done with: the book is the
argument at length, ONTOK is the record written as code, TCA is software
built from the record.

## The reader

An operations, technology or transformation leader in a brownfield, often
regulated enterprise. They arrive from a pilot that underperformed and a
postmortem that blamed the model, or from the book or the repositories,
wanting to know who is behind them. They have seen every operating model and
change program; hype and threat both read as amateur to them. They know
their own organization in particulars: which approval is really a phone call,
which definition has three answers, which manager spends the day relaying.
That knowledge is what the page works on. Every claim is checked by the
reader against their own case, so the page never needs a case of its own.

## The outcome

The reader leaves able to state the seed in their own words, recognizing
the five decisions of the cycle as decisions only they can make, and holding
the question _which stream first_. They want the person who taught it
because the teaching was the demonstration of the capability. That is how the
page sells: a teacher is a knowledge consultant. Nothing is held back for a
conversation. The door is one action, in the bar and under the founder's
name, and the teaching never points at it.

## The arc

The page is **TAL's positions**: what it holds to be true about organizations
now. It is not a portfolio, a services menu, or a résumé. It is also a page,
and does the work any page does: stops the reader, gives them a reason to
go on, builds, turns, pays off, and shows the way out. Each sheet has one job
in that arc and leaves the reader holding one question, which the next sheet
answers.

1. **Cover: the stop.** The claim, _AI can only run what’s written down_, is a
   sentence the reader checks against their own pilot. The consequence names
   the two reasons things go unwritten (too much to write; it would name a
   rule someone breaks) and the last line names what happened to them. The
   index, _What AI exposes_, lists the four positions by their short names.
   Leaves: _what wasn't written?_
2. **Positions 01–04: the build.** Each is a claim, a drawn proof, and at most
   one line for what the proof cannot show. In sequence they are the anatomy
   of a decision, in the order the reader meets it: there are two versions of
   every rule (01); the written part is who decides and up to where (02); and
   on which facts (03); and the unwritten part was being carried by
   management (04). Each consequence line must leave the reader holding the
   next sheet's question: 01 _then what can an agent be given?_, 02 _deciding
   on what?_, 03 _and the people who were carrying?_, 04 _how is it done?_
3. **Sheet 05, What it takes: the turn.** The page flips from what is exposed
   to what to do. The headline is the method in one line, and the reading
   line under it is the four positions assembled into the seed: _only what is
   written_ (01), _who decides, up to where_ (02), _on which facts_ (03),
   _everything else a person supplies by judgment_ (04). The reader should
   recognize the sentence as the one they have just been taught. Then the
   cycle, taught whole, each stage ending in the question the next answers.
   This is the deepest content on the page and where a skimming reader would
   leave; the stage's three reading speeds (see DESIGN.md) are the defense.
   Leaves, from Scale: _which stream is next?_, which returns to See.
4. **Work: the payoff.** Where the method is practiced, for the reader who
   will vet it: the book, ONTOK, and TCA, each standing on its own.
5. **Founder: the way out.** Who he is, declared, and the door: one action and
   the address.

### Current positions

- Every company runs twice: on paper, and in practice.
- Authority no one wrote down can’t be handed to an agent.
- People quietly fix what systems disagree on. Agents can’t.
- Much of management is carrying decisions between people who can’t see the
  whole.

## What it takes: the cycle

The sheet teaches the method whole and sells only by the reader coming to
know it. Each stage (See, Declare, Refactor, Prototype, Scale) is set out as
a demonstration and carries:

1. **A premise** the reader can check against their own organization.
2. **The reasoning**: why the premise forces this stage, derived from the
   seed and keyed to the position sheets it rests on, never from a case, a
   company, or a hypothetical. One term is defined here and marked.
3. **The decision**: the one thing this stage fixes. Where what is left open
   is part of the decision, the line says so.
4. **The rules**, each with its reason, as the sheet's notes. As many as the
   stage needs; never three by reflex. Every rule carries its _because_; a
   rule without its reason is an instruction, not teaching.
5. **What it earns**: the conclusion, as the stage's consequence line.
6. **The next question** the reader's own reasoning asks, set as the way on.

The subject of every sentence is the work, never TAL. Each stage is a
decision only the sponsor can make, and the page says so where the stage
turns on it.

## Voice: quiet authority

- **Declare.** Plain statements about the world, as fact. No hedging, no hype.
- **Never describe TAL or the founder.** No adjectives about quality, rarity,
  or expertise. No "leading", "expert", "one of the few".
- **No one speaks for TAL on the page.** No _we_, _our_, _I_. The subject of
  every sentence is the organization, the work, or the reader's own case; the
  founder is named in the third person.
- **Offense, not defense.** Name the capability that becomes possible, not the
  failure prevented. Avoid defining value by _never_, _cannot_, _without_,
  except where the limit itself is the content (what an agent can hold).
- **No size or modesty words.** No _independent_, _small_, _boutique_.
- **No selling.** What it takes teaches the work; it is not a services
  list. No tiers, no per-product asks, no stage that points at the door.
- **No performed significance.** The remove-claudeish skill
  (`.agents/skills/remove-claudeish/SKILL.md`) is run on every copy change.
  It removes performance, never content: when a pattern it names and a line
  the ledger holds conflict, the content stays and is reworded.

### Kept compressions

Seven lines are short on purpose and are not to be unpacked or trimmed by a
rhythm rule. Each is a claim the reader can test, not a line built to be
quoted:

1. _AI can only run what’s written down._ (cover)
2. _Every company runs twice: on paper, and in practice._ (01)
3. _People quietly fix what systems disagree on. Agents can’t._ (03)
4. _What remains is leadership._ (04)
5. _Automating one step of a tangled stream only tangles it faster._
   (Refactor)
6. _it will run twice until it has run once_ (Prototype)
7. The founder's mirrored sentence: every decision about an organization is a
   design for its software, and the reverse.

## Say the unsaid, with dignity

What lands with this reader is the plain truth they already suspect:

- Some rules go unwritten because there was too much to write; some because no
  one wanted to say them. Name both, once, as mechanism.
- Making the organization explicit changes management. Say what coordination
  was and what leadership remains, with no villains and no flattery. The
  reader should recognize the work they already wished they were doing.
- State consequences plainly; never perform candor.

## Show, don't tell

Making the reader feel seen is showing: name a particular they have lived and
would recognize (five answers to one question, a pilot no customer noticed),
then show the act that changes it. Benefit words and aphorisms about value are
telling, however well phrased.

- Proofs and reasoning are from **first principles**: no case studies, no
  company names, no statistics, no history, no hypotheticals. An instance
  (an order placed, a claim filed, the one who knows to call finance) is a
  particular the reader supplies from their own organization, not a case.
- **Never put the founder's résumé on the page**, and never dress it up as
  evidence. A powerful person doesn't list achievements.
- Never fill a slot by **copying existing text** (book lines, posts, repo
  descriptions). Synthesize what it means and say it newly.
- The founder is identified by a **declaration of how he thinks**, not by his
  record, his output, quotations, or praise:

  > **Kyle J. Tobin** founded TAL to build organizations the way they will now
  > have to run: whole, explicit, and executable, from the authority at the top
  > to the code underneath. He thinks in both directions at once.

## Less is more, quality over quantity

- Each element has one job; say each idea once. Where the same fact appears in
  the decision line and a note, the note carries the reason and the decision
  carries the fact.
- The headline argues, the proof shows, one line at most adds the rest.
- Choose the strongest few; leave out what is merely true.
- Teaching is not padding. A word that carries the reason or the nuance stays;
  the test is whether the reader could reconstruct the step without it.

## The works

They share a conviction, not a pipeline. Never present them as layers of one
system.

- **The Automated Organization** (book): the organization is the thing being
  transformed. Find the organization that actually operates; make its
  authority explicit enough to delegate; govern only what binds.
- **ONTOK** (open-source language): the record, written as code. A closed core
  every organization refines into its own kinds; a fact exists only if it is
  proven.
- **Type Construction Architecture** (open-source method): software built from
  the record, correct by construction. A type is both the limit and the
  instruction, so agents build to it at full speed.

## Working rules learned the hard way

- An instruction is a delta on this record, never a replacement for it.
  Before a change, name what already fixed the change could reach; after,
  check each of those and report what held and what moved.
- Content wins over rule. When a voice or rhythm rule would remove a fact the
  ledger holds, reword to keep the fact.
- Do the synthesis. Commit to a judgment; don't hide behind lists, quotes,
  records, or questions.
- Evolve the existing design; never wipe and restart.
- Make decisions inside the domain instead of handing them back.
- Copy is verified like layout: after every change the whole page is read, top
  to bottom, at reading pace, as a stranger would, and the read is reported.
