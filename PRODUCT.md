# TAL product and positioning

This file is the record the page is built from. It is written before the
page is touched and the page is checked against it, never the other way
round. A change the record cannot account for is a change to the record
first, stated as such.

## What TAL is

**AI can run only the decisions a company has already made.** Most of a
company's decisions are made when the case arrives, by whoever it arrives to.
**TAL makes organizations explicit**: from the authority at the top to the
code underneath.

TAL is a company that consults and builds products. The products and the
consulting are one body of work: the products are the problems met in every
organization, solved once and given away; the engagements apply the same
thinking inside one organization.

## The seed

Everything on the page grows from one model, and every sentence on the page
must be derivable from it:

An organization is a set of decisions. A decision is explicit when it is
determinate: one named holder may make it, the facts it is made on resolve
to one proven answer, and the limit past which it returns to a person is a
condition that can be checked. A rule written for a person to interpret is
not explicit, however much of it is written down; the person completes it
with judgment each time a case arrives, so the decision is made at arrival.
Only a decision made in advance can be run by something other than the
person who would have made it. An organization is executable where its
decisions are explicit, by people and machines alike. Judgment is neither
written down nor removed: it is located at the limits, with the person whose
authority the limit was cut from. A limit is the one form in which authority
can be handed to a machine and taken back, by moving it. Where a decision is
built as a type, the wrong decision cannot be constructed inside its limit.

_Explicit_ never means _written down_ on this page. Documented is not
determinate; code is explicit and a policy is not, and the difference is not
the medium.

The positions are the parts of the seed. The cycle makes one stream
explicit. The works are the instruments: the book is the argument at length,
ONTOK is the organization's kinds and facts as types, TCA is software whose
types are its limits.

## The reader

An operations, technology or transformation leader in a brownfield, often
regulated enterprise. They arrive from a pilot that underperformed and a
postmortem that blamed the model, or from the book or the repositories,
wanting to know who is behind them. They have seen every operating model and
change program; hype and threat both read as amateur to them. They believe
their organization is explicit because it is documented. They know it in
particulars: which approval is really a phone call, which definition has
three answers, which manager spends the day relaying. That knowledge is what
the page works on. Every claim is checked by the reader against their own
case, so the page never needs a case of its own.

## The work done on the reader

In order, each change making the next possible:

1. Move the cause of the stalled pilot from the model to the organization,
   without blame.
2. Change what _explicit_ means to them: documented is not determinate. With
   dignity: the paper was written for people, and people were the resolver,
   which was right until something other than a person tried to run it.
3. Give them the decision as the unit of sight: who may make it, on which
   facts, up to where.
4. Change what a fact is: proven, not asserted.
5. Show that authority transfers in exactly one form, a limit, which they
   already govern by at their best.
6. Locate judgment instead of eliminating it: at the limits, with the person
   whose judgment it is. The fear about people is answered by structure.
7. Make the workaround honorable: the person who overrode the rule was
   running the organization, and declaring practice the true version is a
   decision the sponsor makes.
8. Show correct by construction for an organization: inside a limit built as
   a type, the wrong decision cannot be made. The executable organization is
   the hope, and it is concrete.
9. Collapse the split between the organization and its software.
10. Hand them the five decisions as theirs.
11. Earn authority by never asking for it: every claim is checkable.
12. Leave them holding one question: which stream first.

Never: sold to, threatened, flattered, told they were wrong, or handed a
hypothetical about someone else's company.

## The outcome

The reader leaves able to state the seed in their own words, recognizing
the five decisions of the cycle as decisions only they can make, and holding
the question _which stream first_. They want the person who taught it
because the teaching was the demonstration of the capability. That is how the
page sells: a teacher is a knowledge consultant. Nothing is held back for a
conversation. The door is one action, in the bar and under the founder's
name, and the teaching never points at it.

## Show, never caption

Everything valuable on the page has a drawn form in the page's own proof
grammar: a table, a struck line or cell, a hatched column, a code block, a
stream line. Where a thing is drawn, no sentence restates it. A consequence
line exists only for what the proof cannot show; a note exists only for what
the plate cannot show. The cycle uses the same grammar as the positions, and
its plates reuse the positions' instances (the refund and its limit, the
active customer, the policy exception), so the reader meets the same decision
being made explicit stage by stage.

## The arc

The page is **TAL's positions**: what it holds to be true about organizations
now. It is not a portfolio, a services menu, or a résumé. Each sheet has one
job and leaves the reader holding one question, which the next sheet answers.

1. **Cover.** The claim, _AI can run only the decisions a company has already
   made_, checked against their own pilot. One line under it: most decisions
   are made when the case arrives, by whoever it arrives to. The index,
   _What AI exposes_, lists the four positions. Leaves: _which decisions
   haven't been made?_
2. **Positions 01–04.** Each is a claim and a drawn proof. In sequence they
   are the anatomy of a decision: every rule exists twice, as written and as
   made (01); the only authority a machine can hold is a limit, moved to
   hand over or take back (02); a fact is one proven definition, or a person
   chooses between three (03); carrying decisions between people who cannot
   see the whole was most of management, and setting limits is what remains
   (04). Leaves, from 04: _how is it done?_
3. **Sheet 05, What it takes.** The page turns from what is exposed to what
   to do. The headline is the method in one line: make one stream's
   decisions before its cases arrive. Then the cycle, each stage a premise,
   a plate drawn in the proof grammar, the reasoning keyed to the sheets it
   rests on, notes for what the plate cannot show, and the question the next
   stage answers. Scale's question returns to See.
4. **Work.** The instruments, for the reader who will vet them: the book,
   ONTOK, and TCA, each standing on its own.
5. **Founder.** Who he is, in one sentence, and the door: one action and the
   address.

### Current positions

- Every company runs twice: on paper, and in practice.
- The only authority an agent can hold is a limit.
- People quietly fix what systems disagree on. Agents can't.
- Much of management is carrying decisions between people who can't see the
  whole.

## What it takes: the cycle

The sheet teaches the method whole and sells only by the reader coming to
know it. Each stage (See, Declare, Refactor, Prototype, Scale) is set out as
a demonstration:

1. **A premise** the reader can check against their own organization.
2. **The plate**: the artifact the stage produces, drawn in the proof
   grammar. The decision the stage fixes is visible in the plate, never
   captioned.
3. **The reasoning**: why the premise forces this stage, derived from the
   seed and keyed to the position sheets it rests on. One term is defined
   here and marked.
4. **The notes**: only what the plate cannot show, each rule with its reason.
   As many as the stage needs; never three by reflex.
5. **The next question** the reader's own reasoning asks, set as the way on.

The subject of every sentence is the work, never TAL. Each stage is a
decision only the sponsor can make, and the plate shows it made.

## Voice: quiet authority

- **Declare.** Plain statements about the world, as fact. No hedging, no hype.
- **Never describe TAL or the founder.** No adjectives about quality, rarity,
  or expertise. No "leading", "expert", "one of the few".
- **No one speaks for TAL on the page.** No _we_, _our_, _I_. The subject of
  every sentence is the organization, the work, or the reader's own case; the
  founder is named in the third person.
- **Never _written down_ for _explicit_.** Determinate, made in advance,
  proven, made explicit: the word is chosen for the mechanism it names.
- **Offense, not defense.** Name the capability that becomes possible, not the
  failure prevented, except where the limit itself is the content.
- **No size or modesty words.** No _independent_, _small_, _boutique_.
- **No selling.** What it takes teaches the work; it is not a services
  list. No tiers, no per-product asks, no stage that points at the door.
- **No performed significance.** The remove-claudeish skill
  (`.agents/skills/remove-claudeish/SKILL.md`) is run on every copy change.
  It removes performance, never content: when a pattern it names and a line
  the ledger holds conflict, the content stays and is reworded.

### Kept compressions

Lines that are short on purpose and are not to be unpacked or trimmed by a
rhythm rule. Each is a claim the reader can test, not a line built to be
quoted:

1. _Every company runs twice: on paper, and in practice._ (01)
2. _People quietly fix what systems disagree on. Agents can't._ (03)
3. _Automating one step of a tangled stream only tangles it faster._
   (Refactor)
4. _it will run twice until it has run once_ (Prototype)

## Say the unsaid, with dignity

What lands with this reader is the plain truth they already suspect:

- The workaround was the organization running. Say so once, as mechanism,
  and make reporting it a decision to record rather than a rule broken.
- Making the organization explicit changes management. Say what coordination
  was and what leadership remains, with no villains and no flattery. The
  reader should recognize the work they already wished they were doing.
- State consequences plainly; never perform candor.

## Show, don't tell

Making the reader feel seen is showing: name a particular they have lived and
would recognize (three answers to one question, a pilot no customer noticed),
then show the act that changes it. Benefit words and aphorisms about value are
telling, however well phrased.

- Proofs and reasoning are from **first principles**: no case studies, no
  company names, no statistics, no history, no hypotheticals. An instance
  (a refund, a claim filed, the one who knows to call finance) is a
  particular the reader supplies from their own organization, not a case.
- **Never put the founder's résumé on the page**, and never dress it up as
  evidence. A powerful person doesn't list achievements.
- Never fill a slot by **copying existing text** (book lines, posts, repo
  descriptions). Synthesize what it means and say it newly.
- The founder is identified by **one declaration**, and the page above it is
  how he thinks:

  > **Kyle J. Tobin** founded TAL to build organizations the way they will now
  > have to run: whole, explicit, and executable, from the authority at the top
  > to the code underneath.

## Less is more, quality over quantity

- Each element has one job; say each idea once. Where a plate shows a fact,
  no line restates it.
- The headline argues, the proof shows, one line at most adds the rest.
- Choose the strongest few; leave out what is merely true.
- Teaching is not padding. A word that carries the reason or the nuance stays;
  the test is whether the reader could reconstruct the step without it.

## The works

They share a conviction, not a pipeline. Never present them as layers of one
system.

- **The Automated Organization** (book): the organization is the thing being
  made executable, not its software. Find the one that operates; make its
  decisions determinate; govern at the limits.
- **ONTOK** (open-source language): the organization's kinds as types and its
  facts as values that exist only once proven. A closed core every
  organization refines into its own kinds.
- **Type Construction Architecture** (open-source method): software whose
  types are its limits. What a type does not admit cannot be built, so an
  agent working from the types works inside the limits.

## Working rules learned the hard way

- An instruction is a delta on this record, never a replacement for it.
  Before a change, name what already fixed the change could reach; after,
  check each of those and report what held and what moved.
- Content wins over rule. When a voice or rhythm rule would remove a fact the
  ledger holds, reword to keep the fact.
- The answers come from reasoning and deciding here, not from other sources.
- Do the synthesis. Commit to a judgment; don't hide behind lists, quotes,
  records, or questions.
- Evolve the existing design; never wipe and restart.
- Make decisions inside the domain instead of handing them back.
- Copy is verified like layout: after every change the whole page is read, top
  to bottom, at reading pace, as a stranger would, and the read is reported.
