The CRM has a customer, billing has another, support has a third, and someone reconciles them by hand. Each system rebuilt its own piece of what the organization is, because there was nowhere else to keep it. An agent inherits whichever piece it is given.

ONTOK is that place, and it is built with Type Construction Architecture. What a customer, a payment, a role or a rule is gets written once, as your organization means it, and that definition is itself running code every system and agent works from. It belongs to the organization, never to a vendor’s system.

So the organization’s own definitions behave like its rules: readable by the people who own them, and unable to hold anything that was never proven. A payment that names no real customer is never recorded, and there is nothing to reconcile later. What the book finds in your people becomes something every system can hold.
